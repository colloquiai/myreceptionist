When a caller cannot be put through, or would rather leave a message, the receptionist takes one. It asks for three things, reads them back, and then sends the message by email or text. This page explains each step and what the person receiving the message gets.

## When the receptionist takes a message

The receptionist takes a message when:

- the caller asks to leave a message or to be called back, in any words. This wins over everything else, even if they also name a person;
- the office is closed and the caller says yes to the offer in the closed or public holiday greeting;
- a call could not be put through, or the person they asked for is not available, and the caller chooses to leave a message.

If you chose **Say the greeting, then end the call** for calls outside office hours or on a public holiday, callers at those times cannot leave a message. See [Closed and public holidays](/on-a-call/closed-and-public-holidays).

## What the receptionist asks for

The receptionist collects three things, one question at a time:

1. **The caller's name.** If they gave it earlier in the call, it does not ask again.
2. **A number to call them back on.** It always asks for the best number to call them back on. It does not use the number the caller is ringing from, even if they say "this one": it asks them to say the number. Then it reads the number back one digit at a time and asks them to confirm it.
3. **The message.** If they said it earlier in the call, it does not ask again.

It asks for each thing at most twice. If the caller still does not give their name, the message goes without one. If they do not give a message, the message is "please call back".

### No number, no message

The receptionist will not take a message without a callback number. If the caller cannot or will not give one, it says:

> "I'm sorry, I can't take a message without a number for the team to call you back on. Please call again when you have one, or during office hours."

Then it says goodbye and ends the call.

## Reading it back

Before it sends anything, the receptionist reads the details back:

> "Just to confirm: your name is Sam Lee, your number is 0 4 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8, and your message is please call me about my invoice. Is that correct?"

If the caller gave no name, it leaves the name out. If the caller corrects something, it reads the details back again. If the caller changes their mind and does not want to leave the message after all, it says goodbye and ends the call.

## Where the message goes

Where the message goes depends on who the receptionist last tried to put the caller through to on this call, and on that person's **Send their messages by** setting on the [People and teams](/receptionist/people-and-teams) card.

| The caller was trying to reach | The message goes |
|---|---|
| A person set to **Text to their mobile** | By text to that person's mobile |
| A person set to **Email** | By email to that person, with a copy to your catch-all contact |
| A person set to **Catch-all contact only** | By email to your catch-all contact |
| Nobody yet (for example, the caller asked to leave a message straight away, or the office was closed) | By email to your catch-all contact |

"Trying to reach" means someone the receptionist actually tried to put the call through to, for example a person who was not available or did not answer. A person the caller only named, such as when the office was closed, does not count: that message goes to the catch-all, with the person's name in it.

## What the message says

### An email

The subject is "Message for" the person's name, "from" the caller's name, for example "Message for Dr Patel from Sam Lee". The email lists:

- **Message for**: the person the message is for. When it goes to the catch-all because the caller was not put through to anyone, this is who the caller asked for, or "the team" if they named nobody.
- **Caller name**: the name the caller gave, or "not given".
- **Callback number**: the number the caller said and confirmed.
- **Called from**: the number the caller rang from.
- **Message**: the message, in the caller's words.

### A text

A text looks like this:

> Message for Dr Patel from Sam Lee. Callback number: 0 4 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8. Message: please call me about my invoice (taken by Ava, Bayside Dental)

"Ava" is the **Receptionist's name** and "Bayside Dental" is the **Business name**. A text does not include the number the caller rang from, and it is not copied to the catch-all contact.

## What the caller hears at the end

When the message has gone, the caller hears one of these, then the goodbye:

- If it went to a person: "Your message for Dr Patel has been passed along, and they'll get back to you."
- If it went to the catch-all: "Your message has been passed along, and someone from the team will follow up soon."

If the message could not be sent, the caller hears this, then the goodbye:

> "I'm sorry, I'm having trouble getting your message through right now. Please call back during business hours."

<Warning>
Your catch-all contact must have an email address. Messages that go to the catch-all, and copies of emailed messages, are sent to it.
</Warning>

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why does the receptionist ask for a number when it can see the caller's number?">
    It is told always to ask, so the person calling back has the number the caller wants to be reached on. The number they rang from is still included in an emailed message, as **Called from**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does the person get a copy of the recording?">
    No. The message contains only the details listed above. Recordings and transcripts are on the [Calls](/calls/call-history) page.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a caller ask to be put through instead of leaving a message?">
    Yes. While the receptionist is taking a message, the caller can ask to be put through to someone. During office hours it puts them through; when the office is closed it explains it cannot.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
