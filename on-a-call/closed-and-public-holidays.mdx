When someone rings while your business is closed, the receptionist tells them so and, unless you choose otherwise, offers to take a message. This page explains how the receptionist knows you are closed and what happens with each choice on the [Opening hours](/receptionist/opening-hours) card.

## How the receptionist knows the time

At the start of every call, before it says anything, the receptionist checks the time, day and date in your business's timezone. That is the **Timezone** on the [Your business](/receptionist/your-business) card. Your **Office hours**, your **Public holidays** and each person's **Available** time all go by that timezone.

It checks, in this order:

1. **Is today in your Public holidays list?** If so, the caller hears the public holiday greeting.
2. **Is it within your Office hours today?** If not, the caller hears the closed greeting.
3. Otherwise the office is open and the call goes ahead as usual. See [How a call goes](/on-a-call/how-a-call-goes).

Only the dates you add to **Public holidays** count as holidays. The receptionist does not know about any other holidays.

<Warning>
If the **Timezone** is wrong, the receptionist will think you are open when you are closed, or the other way round. Check it first if callers hear the wrong greeting.
</Warning>

## How your hours are said

The greetings say your hours in words, written for you from your **Office hours**. For example, Monday to Friday 9:00 to 17:00 is said as "Monday to Friday, nine to five". With a Saturday morning added it becomes "Monday to Friday, nine to five, and Saturday, nine to twelve".

## Outside office hours

The **Outside office hours** row on the **Opening hours** card has two choices.

{/* SHOT: a receptionist's page, the Opening hours card, showing the Office hours, Public holidays, During office hours, Outside office hours and On a public holiday rows */}

### Offer to take a message

This is the standard choice. With the standard wording, the caller hears:

> "Thank you for calling Harbour Plumbing. I'm sorry, the office is closed at the moment. We're open Monday to Friday, nine to five, excluding public holidays. Would you like to leave a message so someone can call you back?"

Then the receptionist follows the caller:

| The caller | What happens |
|---|---|
| Says yes, or starts giving their name, number or message | It takes a message. See [Taking messages](/on-a-call/taking-messages). |
| Asks a question, such as your opening hours or services | It answers. See [Answering questions](/on-a-call/answering-questions). |
| Asks to be put through to someone | It says "I'm sorry, I can't put you through while the office is closed. Would you like to leave a message?" |
| Says no thanks, they will call back, or goodbye | It says goodbye and ends the call. |
| Says anything else | It takes a message. |

### Say the greeting, then end the call

The caller hears a closed greeting with no question in it, then the goodbye, and the call ends. With the standard wording:

> "You've reached Harbour Plumbing. The office is closed at the moment. We're open Monday to Friday, nine to five, excluding public holidays."

The caller cannot talk over this greeting, leave a message or ask anything.

<Tip>
If you write your own closed greeting with this choice, do not end it with a question. The caller will not be able to answer it.
</Tip>

## On a public holiday

The **On a public holiday** row works the same way, with its own greeting. It only matters if you have added at least one date to **Public holidays**.

### Offer to take a message

With the standard wording, the caller hears:

> "Thank you for calling Bayside Dental. I'm sorry, the office is closed today for the public holiday. We're open Monday to Friday, nine to five. Would you like to leave a message so someone can call you back?"

After that, the call goes exactly as it does outside office hours: a message, an answer to a question, or a goodbye. A caller who asks to be put through hears the same "I'm sorry, I can't put you through while the office is closed" line.

### Say the greeting, then end the call

With the standard wording, the caller hears:

> "You've reached Bayside Dental. The office is closed today for the public holiday. We're open Monday to Friday, nine to five."

Then the goodbye, and the call ends. The caller cannot talk over the greeting.

## During office hours

The **During office hours** row decides what happens while you are open: **The receptionist answers the call**, or **Put every call straight through** to one person. See [Putting calls through](/on-a-call/putting-calls-through). Your choice there does not change what happens outside office hours or on a public holiday.

## Changing the wording

You can write your own **Greeting when the office is closed** and **Greeting on a public holiday** on the [What it says](/receptionist/what-it-says) card. In your own wording, `{hours}` is replaced with your hours in words, `{firm}` with your business name and `{persona}` with the receptionist's name.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can a caller still be put through after hours?">
    No. Outside office hours and on public holidays, the receptionist does not put calls through to anyone. It offers to take a message instead.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if the office closes while someone is on the call?">
    The receptionist checks the time once, when it answers the call. A call answered just before closing time is treated as a call during office hours for the rest of the call.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I need to add public holidays every year?">
    Yes. Only the exact dates in your **Public holidays** list count. Add each year's dates on the [Opening hours](/receptionist/opening-hours) card.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a caller ask a question when the office is closed?">
    Yes, with **Offer to take a message**. After the closed greeting the caller can ask a question, and the receptionist answers it in the usual way. With **Say the greeting, then end the call**, they cannot.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
